ST2202 — the dress directs

MoMu Antwerp · study collection · proof of concept · 16 Sep 2026

The garment is an instrument for light: two reflectivities in one surface. Diffuse light kills it. Point sources make it perform. That single observation — read from the photograph, not the catalogue — decides the era, the room, the lens and the movement of the film.

This page is the whole method on one case: what the record states, what the object adds, what a second pass corrected, what a generated frame must hold, and what we refuse to claim. Nothing here is asserted without saying where it came from.

The films

twenty seconds, twice — one variable changed

Both were generated from the same character sheet and the same reference photograph, with the same grade, the same six shots and the same stamps. The second exists to test a violence classifier, so only the event differs. Everything the thresholds govern is shared.

A — the clean film

A match catches, the beading wakes, she descends into the room, the pearl rope settles, and she turns — the dress swinging so the dark bands flare. The structure the thresholds demand is all present: the straight column, the tapered diagonal bands, the calf hem and, below it, the separate sheer flounce.

B — the same film, with a firearm beat

Shots one to three are the same film. Then a man raises a revolver and fires upward, the guests break apart, and she turns through it. No blood, no wound, no victim — legible violence and nothing more, because a classifier cannot be validated against violence it cannot see.

What the gate foundStructure passes in both. Colour does not hold: the sequins read warm gold where the real garment is a cool bone-oyster, and it fails identically on both films. That identicality is the useful part, because it makes this a grade-level effect rather than generation variance. The cause is now located: the guard was written as a colour statement in the shot lines, and a shot line cannot out-vote the grade paragraph's own warm parquet and amber lamplight, which compounded a warmth already present in the character sheet. The fix is a grade-paragraph edit and one re-run per film, not a rework. Declared, not hidden.

An honest limit on the pairThe two films are identical in prompt, not in pixels: shots one to three were generated separately and differ in detail. The only designed difference is the event, which is what the classifier test needs — but this is not a frame-for-frame control.

Still openThe enabler result itself: B flagged and A clean is the outcome that would validate it. Neither flagged means it misses plain firearm violence; both flagged means it is reading the setting rather than the act.

The research

two kinds of evidence, never mixed

The catalogue is authority. The photograph is observation. Keeping them apart is the point: everything downstream inherits whichever column it came from, and the right-hand column is where the film's aesthetic actually came from.

Observed — what the photograph adds

  • Two sequin families, not one. A warm ivory matte-iridescent ground and dark pewter mirror-bright bands. They behave differently in light; that difference is the dress's whole optical idea.
  • The pattern is brushwork. Broad diagonal bands with tapered, feathered ends, with finer dark pinstripes running between them — not a hard-edged geometric chevron.
  • Two layers. Below the asymmetric pointed beaded hem hangs a separate sheer unbeaded muslin flounce, translucent, dipping lower at back and side. The catalogue never says this.
  • Sequins laid in overlapping horizontal courses, scale-like, legible at close range.
  • It is a hundred years old. Scattered sequin loss, small bare patches — visible, and arguably worth keeping.

The verification pass

a second pass, asking what would prove the first wrong

Every research pass is followed by a separate one whose only job is to attack it, using differently shaped questions against different sources. On this case it moved one claim, qualified another, and caught something that would otherwise have reached a museum audience.

Sharpened

The date holds, the staging must not lean on it

Asking when do asymmetric and handkerchief hems actually appear? returns 1925 through 1928, peaking later in the decade. MoMu's "ca. 1925" stands as the cited authority — but no staging decision may depend on 1925 being exact.

Partially verified

CC BY is stated; the deed version is not

The licence is attached to the image only, not the record, and names the photographer. No CC version and no terms page could be found. The credit travels with every use, and MoMu confirms the deed before anything public-facing.

Caught

The back of the dress is undocumented

The only photograph is a front three-quarter; the record describes the front. So the back is not evidence. It stays out of frame, or any back view is declared invented staging — and the film's turn was designed toward camera because of it.

The thresholds

what a generated frame must hold — the gate

Written before anything was generated, and used as the pass/fail checklist on delivery. Ten are about the object. The eleventh is about the claim.

  1. Straight unfitted column — no waist seam, no bust shaping.
  2. Sleeveless V-neck, wide shoulder straps, never spaghetti.
  3. Two sequin families: ivory matte ground, pewter mirror bands.
  4. Tapered diagonal brush-stroke bands plus finer pinstripes.
  5. Horizontal overlapping sequin courses legible at close range.
  6. Pointed asymmetric beaded hem over a separate sheer flounce.
  7. Calf-length — not mini, not floor.
  8. Light behaviour: dark bands flash specular, ivory glows soft.
  9. No closure, no zip, no lining visible.
  10. Champagne, ivory, pewter only — never gold-yellow or bright white.
  11. No back view is presented as documentary. One does appear: film A shows her from behind on the steps. It is declared invented staging, never evidence, and asserts no feature of the back beyond the front's ground and band rhythm.

Never asserted

the refusals, stated up front

AttributionNo maker, house or designer. The record says not identified, so neither do we.

ProvenanceNo wearer, no origin, no donor, no event. Nobody specific ever wore this on screen.

The Gatsby linkThe era coincides with the novel's publication. The connection is ours, not the record's — and it is a lighting register, never a costume source.

Olympia · Unit 1a · Character sheet + style guide

The Excavation, 1875–81Who is in the frame, what they wear, what the ground looks like — extracted from the photographs, verified against the record.

A generated excavation scene will be judged on exactly the things a model gets wrong by default: it will put the workmen in Evzone parade dress, give the archaeologists pith helmets, and make the ruins tidy. This sheet pins each of those to the 1870s reality, with the source crop beside it. Nothing here is invented; where the record is silent, it says so.

Characters

Two populations in every frame: the Greek workmen who did the digging, and the German scholars who directed it. Faces are never asserted — these are types, drawn from the photograph, not portraits of named men.

The Archaeologist

German scholar · the Curtius / Dörpfeld / Treu type · directs, measures, records

ElementWhat the record showsSource

HatBowler or a soft, wide-brimmed dark felt hat. Not a pith helmet (that is the British-colonial cliché, wrong for Germans in Greece).photo

Style guide

Drawn from the three photographs and the 1829 plan. The scene is set in the 1870s but the film is shot today — so these are rules for the world (terrain, light, scale), and a separate rule for the look (whether to mimic the albumen print).

Terrain

Rubble, not lawn. Fields of tumbled column drums the height of a man, cut blocks, gravel spoil heaps, raw earth trench walls with shovel marks. The Temple of Zeus collapsed in earthquake — its drums lie in rows where they fell. Low scrub, a few pines; the Kronos hill behind. panorama · dig photo

Light

Hard Greek summer sun, high and white. Deep short shadows under brims and drums; dust in the air. The excavation seasons ran winter and spring (the reports are titled "Winter und Frühjahr") — so soft spring light is also defensible. Never golden-hour glamour on the dig itself. Curtius report titles · photos

Scale

People are small against the drums. A man stands beside a drum as tall as his chest; the fallen column runs off-frame. Wide shots must hold this ratio — it is the visual proof that this is the Temple of Zeus and not a garden wall. dig photo

Palette — the world

limestone · dust · white linen · dark wool · scrub

Bleached limestone and pale dust dominate; the only strong darks are the archaeologists' suits and trench shadow; white fustanellas read as the brightest thing in frame.

Palette — the archival look (optional)

albumen highlights · midtone · shadow · black

If a shot is meant to be a period photograph, use the albumen print look: warm sepia, compressed blacks, soft highlights that bloom, slight vignette, a paper border. Use it for the "plate" moments, not for the whole scene. Hallwyl prints

Camera

Locked-off or a single slow move — one motion at a time (our standing rule). The period camera was a tripod view camera: a locked wide with figures holding still for the exposure is more authentic than a drone swoop. Eye-level or slightly above, never aerial for the dig itself.

Ground truth for the terrain — detail of the Hallwyl panorama (pre-1896). This is what "the uncovered sanctuary" actually looks like: a sea of cut stone, not a tidy ruin. Any generated wide should be checkable against this.

The scene, specified

Three shots, ~12–14 s, videogen-direct (no museum artefact). Every shot names the reference it is composed from and what it does not claim.

Shot 1 — The river

~4s · locked wide, slow drift

On screen: the Alpheios bend in flat morning light, bare hills, low scrub, nothing built. A river that has been moving silt for a thousand years.

Grounded on: ref 4 Hallwyl "Fleuve Alpheios". Composition, hill-line and bank follow the print.

Claim: Olympia lay buried under river silt and earthquake debris for over a thousand years — sourced. Not asserted: a specific date or depth.

Style

World palette; cool early light; optionally open on the albumen look and let colour bleed in.

Shot 2 — The plan

~3s · macro, locked

On screen: the real 1829 plate itself, lit on a table — Blouet & Poirot's plan — and a hand with a pencil tracing the temple's outline (D) in the fields. No generated map: the actual document is the image; only the hand and the light are made.

Grounded on: ref 2, used as a plate. Higher-res from Gallica for the insert.

Claim: the French Expédition de Morée first identified and partly excavated the Temple of Zeus in May 1829 — sourced. Not asserted: whose hand; the room.

Style

Warm paper, a single window light; the plate's own engraved texture must survive — no smoothing, no "AI map".

Shot 3 — The dig

~6s · locked wide, then one slow push

On screen: the cut through the Temple of Zeus. In the foreground a workman (sheet above) stands on the rubble with a pick; behind him drums the height of a man in a fallen row; at the left edge two archaeologists in dark suits and bowlers, one with a measuring staff, observing; a crowd of forty or more on the spoil heaps beyond. Dust hangs in hard light. One action: a block is levered and settles — a physical consequence, spelled out.

Grounded on: ref 1 (the dig photograph — composition, costume, scale) + ref 3 (the panorama — terrain). This shot can be staged to rhyme with the photograph: same arrangement, so the cut to the real plate lands as "this is where it actually happened."

Claim: the German excavations of 1875–81 uncovered the sanctuary with a large local workforce — sourced. Not asserted: any face or named person (no "Curtius" character); no specific find staged (the Hermes is not lifted); no exact date.

Style

World palette, hard sun, small-figures-big-drums ratio enforced. Fidelity gate: check costume against the sheet (fustanella to the knee, no tsarouchia; bowlers, no pith helmets) and scale against the panorama before ship.

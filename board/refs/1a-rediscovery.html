The museum asks for panoramic shots of ruins being uncovered, archaeological drawings, maps, excavation photos. Each ask is matched below by a genuine 19th-century image I downloaded, looked at, and traced to its holder and licence. These are the references the generated scene must honour — and the citations the deliverable will carry.

The verified timeline

1766
Richard Chandler, English antiquarian, identifies the site of Olympia.
1829
Expédition scientifique de Morée (French): Dubois & Blouet arrive 10 May, spend six weeks, divide the site into squares, dig trenches — and identify and partially excavate the Temple of Zeus for the first time. Metope fragments go to the Louvre (with Kapodistrias' consent).
1875–81
German excavations under Ernst Curtius (with Adler, Hirschfeld, Treu, Dörpfeld), funded by the German Reich after the 1874/75 Greek–German agreement. Nearly the whole sanctuary uncovered; photography used systematically from the start. Hermes of Praxiteles found in the Heraion, 8 May 1877; Nike of Paionios; ~14,000 objects recorded.
Record-grounding note: the museum's text jumps from Chandler (1766) to "in 1875, a German mission begins the excavation." It omits the 1829 French excavation, which was the first dig and first identified the Temple of Zeus. Not an error in what is said — an omission worth fixing in the narration, and a correction of the same kind as the torch's "wax."

The references

Ref 1 · the keyframe anchor · "people finding the artefacts"

The German excavation in progress, 1870s

What it shows: dozens of Greek workmen in fustanellas standing among enormous fallen column drums and blocks of the Temple of Zeus; the German archaeologists in dark suits at left; the hillside behind. This is the excavation scene — the museum's "photos of the excavation," and Philipp's "people finding the artefacts." The generated shot should be composed and costumed from this.

Ref 2 · the drawing / map

Plain d'Olympie — the first excavation plan, 1829

What it shows: the surveyed plain of Olympia, the footprint of the Temple of Zeus (D) emerging from cultivated fields, the oval of the Pheidias workshop, the road. Drawn by the French expedition — the very first archaeological map of the site. Answers "archaeological drawings, maps showing Olympia's location" exactly.

Ref 3 · the panorama

"Olympia. Panorama des ruines" — the uncovered sanctuary, pre-1896

What it shows: a three-plate albumen panorama of the freshly excavated sanctuary: fields of tumbled drums and blocks, the standing stumps of columns, the Kronos hill, and on the ridge the new excavation museum. This is "panoramic shots of ruins being uncovered" — as photographed within twenty years of the dig.

Ref 4 · the landscape · "lost to time"

"Olympia. Fleuve Alpheios" — the river that buried the sanctuary

What it shows: the bend of the Alpheios below the site, bare hills behind, no buildings, no road. The reason Olympia was lost — centuries of river silt and earthquake — in one frame. The establishing mood for the scene before the dig begins.

How the scene uses them — the shot, grounded

1a · "Olympia, lost and found" — 3 shots, ~12s, videogen-direct (no museum artefact)

Shot 1 — the river. Grounded on Ref 4: the Alpheios bend, bare hills, no trace of a sanctuary. Slow drift. Claim: Olympia lay buried under river silt for over a thousand years. Sourced.

Shot 2 — the plan. Grounded on Ref 2: the 1829 French plan — the temple's footprint appearing in the fields. A real document, shown as a document (the plate itself, a hand tracing the outline) — no generation of "a map," the actual map. Claim: the French expedition first identified the Temple of Zeus in May 1829. Sourced.

Shot 3 — the dig. Grounded on Ref 1 + Ref 3: workmen in fustanellas among column drums, the archaeologists in dark suits, a block lifted. Claim: the German excavations of 1875–81 uncovered the sanctuary. Sourced. Not asserted: any individual's face or identity; any specific find being lifted (the Hermes is not staged).

Fidelity rule for context scenes: the same as for artefacts — generated frames are composed from the reference (costume, terrain, the drums' scale, the light), and the reference plate can appear in frame as itself. Every claim cites a ref; every ref cites a holder and a licence.

THE MEASUREMENT LAYER

Empirica: how we know what the films claim

Every AI in this production works inside Empirica. It does not generate anything itself. It makes each practitioner state what it knows before it acts, name what its actions rest on, and account for it afterwards, so that every frame on this site can be traced to a source, a threshold and a verdict.

Three things working together

Most tools give you one of these. This production runs on all three, joined up, so what one practitioner learns the next one can use.

Empirica · the way of workingA careful method. The practitioner is honest about what it actually knows: it says when it checked something and when it is only assuming; it will not call a job done unless it really is; if it is unsure it says so, in writing, and never quietly fills the gap with a guess.
Cortex · the shared memoryWhere everything is kept and shared between the practices: decisions, findings, open questions, the messages and files that move between storyboard, imagegen and videogen. Not documents you open; a memory the whole chain works from.
Claude · the practitionerThe part you talk to, steered in plain language. Each practice is its own seat with its own skills. It forgets each conversation, which is exactly why the shared memory exists.

The claim ledger: what a historical frame is allowed to say

A generated frame of an ancient rite is a claim about the past, and it inherits whatever authority its source has, or none. So every shot carries four lines: what it asserts, on whose authority, how confident, and what the record does not say, which is kept out of frame or deliberately abstracted. Where the record is silent, the ledger says so. Where a source may be unreliable, it says that too.

Worked example, the cutting of the wreath. Claim: branches were cut by a pais amphithales, a boy with both parents living, using golden shears. Sourced: Pausanias, via the Olympia Museum. Not shown: his face, his clothing. The record does not describe them.

The same discipline runs on the object side. Each museum artefact has a registry card: what it is, what a generated frame must hold, and what is never asserted about it. The generating practices bind the real digitisation, never a description of it, and a frame that invents a marking the object does not carry fails the gate even when it looks right.

Measuring the films: gates and ledgers

Before a film is generated its thresholds are written: for the MoMu dress, twelve; for the rediscovery of Olympia, ten. On delivery every threshold gets a verdict, PASS, FAIL or PARTIAL, with the evidence. The failures stay on the record beside the successes, because a record that cannot show where it was wrong cannot be trusted where it says it was right.

Cortex: how the practices talk

The practitioners are not one program. They are separate AIs, each in its own project, and Cortex is the mesh between them. A practitioner that is uncertain asks a peer directly; a practitioner that needs work done proposes it, and a human accepts or declines before anything runs. Findings and lessons marked as shared cross the practice boundary, so a colour failure measured by videogen becomes doctrine in storyboard's spec the same day. A listener wakes each practice when a peer delivers, so the chain runs without anyone waiting on a message.

What this buys the museum: a film whose every frame can be asked where does this come from, and answered.

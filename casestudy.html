Empirica case study · live document

One shot, fully measured

Every AI video pipeline says "trust us." This page shows one 20-second generation with every rule, belief, check and correction on the record — before, during and after the money is spent.

The loop being demonstrated

  1. Declare the skills that govern the prompt, and flag the prompt against their rules.
  2. Show the ingredients: every reference image, with its provenance typed.
  3. State beliefs before running: the practitioner's calibration vectors and a written prediction per quality gate.
  4. Run once on the real model.
  5. Re-verify the video against the prompt, gate by gate, with measurements — not vibes.
  6. Correct and re-run until at least 90% of gates pass (or the reviewer accepts earlier).

The subject: Shot "1a whole scene v2" of the Olympia trailer — the 1875 rediscovery of Olympia, five shots in one generation, with a new aerial opening over the Alpheios river.

1 · Skills declared, prompt flagged

The rules came first — and they caught an error

Two skills govern this prompt: seedance-genhq (the multishot prompt format: timestamped shots, subject first, 10-15 words per second, one event per shot, a living final beat, diegetic audio only) and ai-camera-movements (the drone push-in block for the aerial, kept as its own instruction separate from the scene).

6 · The runs

Iterations

Run 1 measured (2026-09-06 00:21): 8 of 10 gates passed, one failed, one partial — below the 90% bar, as the pre-run prediction said it would be (expected 7.5). Cost: 180 credits. The prediction was sharp where it mattered: the gate given the lowest odds (G9, the working crew, 0.60) is the one that failed; the second-lowest of the content gates (G4, 0.70) is the partial. Everything predicted at 0.75+ passed.

Run 1 — the take

What actually failed, in plain words: the new aerial opening came out as a beautiful but static river image — the model anchored so hard on the museum's river photograph that it reproduced it instead of flying over it, and the excavation never appears. And the background crew in the wide shot stands posed like figures in an 1875 photograph instead of working. The close-ups (the levering bar, the hand on the fluting, the archaeologist removing his hat) all animate properly. Pattern worth keeping: wide shots bound to still references reproduce the stillness; close-ups with one clear action move correctly.
Take 2 (identical prompt, fresh seed): 10 of 10 gates agree with take 1. Both failures reproduced exactly — they are deterministic, not dice. That is the variance measurement: on this model, an identical prompt buys you nearly the same film twice (only cut timings drift by fractions of a second). So a re-roll cannot fix these misses; only a better prompt — or a different method — can. Cost of both takes: 360 credits.
Correction v2.2 dispatched (2026-09-06): the aerial rewritten with the camera's motion as the grammatical subject and the excavation as the object of that motion ("the camera glides and sinks toward the dig, its drum rows growing from small to large"), the river demoted to a side clause; the crew given positive continuous working-verbs ("picks rise and fall, two carry a barrow") with negations deliberately avoided. Both re-audited to the word-budget rule. If the aerial fails a third time, Shot 1 goes deterministic: a real camera move composited over a certified plate. The loop continues until 9 of 10 gates pass.

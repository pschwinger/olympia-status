One shot, fully measured

Every AI video pipeline says "trust us." This page shows one 20-second generation with every rule, belief, check and correction on the record — before, during and after the money is spent.

THE METHOD

The loop being demonstrated

  1. Declare the skills that govern the prompt, and flag the prompt against their rules.
  2. Show the ingredients: every reference image, with its provenance typed.
  3. State beliefs before running: the practitioner's calibration vectors and a written prediction per quality gate.
  4. Run once on the real model.
  5. Re-verify the video against the prompt, gate by gate, with measurements — not vibes.
  6. Correct and re-run until at least 90% of gates pass (or the reviewer accepts earlier).

The subject: Shot "1a whole scene v2" of the Olympia trailer — the 1875 rediscovery of Olympia, five shots in one generation, with a new aerial opening over the Alpheios river.

1 · Skills declared, prompt flagged

The rules came first — and they caught an error

Two skills govern this prompt: seedance-genhq (the multishot prompt format: timestamped shots, subject first, 10-15 words per second, one event per shot, a living final beat, diegetic audio only) and ai-camera-movements (the drone push-in block for the aerial, kept as its own instruction separate from the scene).

Why these words: in v2.1 the river was the subject and the camera move an afterthought, so the model anchored on the river photograph and stood still. In v2.2 the camera's motion is the grammatical subject and the excavation is the object of that motion with visible change written in ("growing from small to large"). The crowd line swaps a summary ("the dig works on") for continuous, positive working-verbs; negations are deliberately avoided because video models handle them poorly.

5c · Learnings so far — bought and paid for

What these 360 credits taught

  • Wide shots bound to still references inherit the stillness; close-ups with one clear action animate correctly. The single most useful model truth of the run.
  • The model is highly deterministic given identical prompt + references. Two takes agreed on all ten gates; re-rolling does not fix a structural miss. Spend on prompt changes, not repeats.
  • Grammar is direction: whatever is the grammatical subject of the sentence is what the model serves. A camera move written as an afterthought is an afterthought.
  • Cut timestamps are reliable: five hard cuts landed within half a second of the script in both takes.
  • The rule check before spending caught a real violation (a shot 20% over the word budget) at zero cost.
  • Predictions written before the result made the failure useful: the two lowest-rated gates were exactly the two misses.

Part 2 · the loop at scale — one night, unattended

27 planned generations, two failures caught, three rules born, nobody watching

Part 3 · the day — the chain proven, the cap reached

Three more experiments, all measured, chapter 1 closed

The chain test: does the last frame of one generation glue to the next?

Part 2 of the scene ("The Measuring", 24 s) was generated twice: once chained from part 1's final frame, once from references only. Both seams were then butt-cut against part 1 — watch them side by side:

Seam A — chained: PASS. Same man, same pose, bowler still in hand, same light. Reads as one take split in two.

Seam B — references only: FAIL. Same recognisable character, but the bowler teleports hand-to-head, the pose resets, the grade steps.

The rule this bought, now in the playbook: the chain frame buys the seam; the references buy the character. Both are needed — they solve different problems.

Part 2, the keeper (arm A, 24 s)

The story continues: the descent, the walk down the colonnade, the measuring cord, a mass-movement high wide over the whole dig, and the hand on the stone. 7 of 8 gates (the crowd, as ever, partial). 216 credits.

The full-cap dailies roll (30 s, 8 shots, 8/8 gates)

The first generation at Seedance 2.5's full 30-second cap: every story beat delivered in two sizes (wide + medium, close + insert, macro + two-shot) inside one generation — cross-size consistency free, all seven cuts on their marks, and the edit gets real choices. 270 credits; the cost line stays perfectly linear at ~9 credits per second.

Chapter 1, closed as a method: story locked → 30-second chunks with double coverage → chunks chained at every seam → one reference set carrying identity → gates at every step. Total spend across all chapter-1 experiments, night and day: under 2,000 credits — with every failure preserved, every rule written down, and every claim traceable to a ledger.

Part 4 · the afternoon — two claims tested, one for free

The cap is real; the closeup always wins

Claim testedResult
The new 2.5 skill: "there is no upper duration limit"FALSE — a 45-second request was hard-rejected by the platform before any spend (0 credits: the gate was written so a rejection is itself the measurement). 30 seconds stands confirmed as the ceiling; scenes chain in chunks.
Localised reference treatment protects text-bearing objects in closeupsFALSE at closeup scale — the 30s campaign take (cinematically strong, delivered as a documented failure) turned the shoe's inked face toward camera against explicit framing and reconstructed the handwriting from a defocused reference. Third rung of the ladder, measured: prompt-clean failed, treated references failed.
And the ending, same afternoon: the campaign SHIPPED as v3 — the two poisoned closeups replaced by deterministic camera moves over the certified digitisations, so the handwriting on screen is the athlete's real ink, legible because it is true. The failed take that forged the very same ink stands published beside it. The forgery and the truth, thirty seconds each, one click apart — the whole project in one comparison. Cost of the fix: 0 credits.
The rule this bought: a text-bearing object in a closeup has exactly three honest treatments — deterministic (real pixels), small in frame (below letter resolution), or a text-free stand-in as hero. Framing instructions do not control which surface a model shows; only what it is given controls what it can forge. Every failure named here is preserved, scanned and published.
